# What's New

## The launcher fills the page, and the current section is obvious
- Your tools now run the full width in four columns, with tiles of varying size
  that fit together. The right-hand strip that sat empty below Recent activity
  is gone — that panel moved to a band underneath.
- The section you are in is a solid block in the sidebar instead of a pale
  wash, so it is findable at a glance rather than only when you look for it.

## Superseded: the tool launcher is a bento now
- The tiles are different sizes and tile together with no leftover gaps. The
  four that carry the product's own argument — Deal Genome, the Assumption
  Ledger, Cultural Compatibility and the Synergy Reality Engine — get bigger
  tiles and larger type, so the launcher says which instruments are the point
  rather than presenting fifteen equal squares.
- The gaps under some cards are gone: cards now fill the row they sit in
  instead of stopping at their own text.

## Superseded: the dashboard home uses the whole width
- Your tools are three across instead of two on a wide screen, and Recent
  activity now sits in the column beside them rather than below. On a laptop the
  right third of the page had been empty.

## Bold text is no longer faked
- Anywhere the product emphasised a phrase in bold — the AI disclaimer on Terms,
  figures in Deal Economics, a handful of confirmations — the browser was
  inventing the bold by smearing the normal letterforms sideways, because the
  typeface has no bold to use. Emphasis now uses the weight the typeface
  actually has, and the app can no longer fake one.

## Dashboard text is a step heavier
- Small text across the dashboard — sidebar items, field labels, captions, table
  cells — was set at the lightest weight the typeface has, which read as thin
  against the new light background. It is one step heavier throughout, and so
  are Profile, Help, What's New and Report a Bug.
- Terms and Privacy are deliberately left as they were: they are long documents,
  and a whole page set heavier reads as shouting.

## The dashboard is neutral now
- The coloured working area is gone. The dashboard is a near-neutral light grey
  with white panels on it — the colour was tiring to look at across a full
  screen, and the panels were tinted blue while the ground was tinted green, so
  the two never settled.
- Colour is now reserved for the things that mean something: severity, the
  marker on the current instrument, and the accents inside panels.

## Superseded: the dashboard is lighter
- The working area is now a mid teal rather than a deep green, with the panels
  on it close to white. Text on it is dark ink instead of light.
- One consequence worth knowing: the dashboard now looks the same whether you
  have light or dark selected. The landing still follows your choice.

## One light, teal, in the middle
- The landing is lit by a single soft glow at the centre of the screen, fading
  out with no edge. The separate rays around it are gone: they left a visible
  gap between the light and its own beams, and far from the centre they read as
  drifting patches rather than as light.
- It is the cool teal of the ground rather than orange. A warm point in the
  middle of a green page read as a second, unrelated system.

## The assumption ledger turns continuously
- The cards no longer advance-and-wait. The row travels at a steady rate and
  never stops, which is what it looked like it was supposed to do all along.

## Superseded: one light on the landing, not four
- The soft coloured patches drifting across the background are gone. The page is
  lit by a single source at the centre and the banding it throws; the rays around
  it are short now, so they read as light near the lamp rather than as haze
  spread across the section.
- The glow behind the four movement diagrams was turned right down. The drawings
  themselves are unchanged — the lines converging on a point, the rings, the
  dots — they simply no longer sit in their own pool of coloured light.

## The assumption ledger rotates faster
- Cards advance every 2.4 seconds instead of 3.8. With three on screen the old
  pause was long enough to read as stopped.

## Superseded: one light on the landing, not four
- Three large coloured glows used to drift across the background on their own
  slow cycles, each a different colour and none of them related to anything
  else on screen. Beside a single light source at the centre they read as
  smudges rather than as light. They are gone; the page is lit by one source
  and the banding it throws.

## The light source is back on the landing
- The opening screen used to have a bright point with rays coming off it, like a
  torch beam. It was removed in an earlier pass because, sitting dead centre, it
  looked like a flaw rather than a light. It is back — moved clear of the
  headline, given a visible core so it reads as a lamp, and with a short fan of
  rays that turns around it.

## The sources fit on a laptop screen again
- The three cited statistics at the bottom of the opening screen were being cut
  off on shorter displays. The opening screen now scales its own top and bottom
  spacing to the height of your window, so everything fits without the space
  between the headline and the sentence below it changing at all.

## The assumption ledger rotates by itself, three at a time
- It was a manual slider in practice: putting your cursor on a card to read it
  stopped the rotation. It now rotates continuously and nothing your pointer
  does interrupts it. The arrows are gone — it does not need driving. The dots
  still jump to a specific assumption, and it carries on from there.
- Choosing Still in the top bar stops it, along with all other motion on the
  site.
- Three assumptions are on screen at once instead of one, so the thing the
  ledger exists to show — that a 91 blocks sign-off while a 38 clears it — is a
  comparison you can see rather than remember.
- Six assumptions now instead of three, including a red flag that is NOT holding
  the deal up because somebody already answered it.

## The "who it's for" cards are all one size
- The six role cards took their height from how much copy each one had, so a
  fanned deck showed cards at five different heights. They are one size now.

## The opening headline has room to breathe
- The two lines of "The deal closes. / The decision record shouldn't." were set
  so tightly that the second line's letters ran into the first line's baseline.
  They now have real space between them.
- The claim and the sentence explaining it sat nearly 200px apart with nothing
  in between, which left the first screen looking half-empty. They read as one
  block now, with the space kept at the edges where it holds the composition.

## Severity labels are readable on the dark theme
- Red-flag, watch and grounded labels across the dashboard — the advancement
  gate notice, unanswered counterarguments, scenario notes, outcome reminders,
  "Saved ✓" — were drawn in the colour meant for bars and borders rather than
  the one meant for words. On the dark theme a red-flag label sat at 3.1:1,
  below the readability standard; it is 5.8:1 now. Nothing changes on the light
  theme, where the two colours are identical — which is why this went unnoticed.

## The sidebar reads as its own layer
- The navigation sidebar and the working area beside it were very nearly the
  same colour, separated only by a hairline. The sidebar now sits a step back
  from the working area on both themes, so the three layers of the dashboard —
  navigation, working area, and the panels in it — each read as their own.
- The marker on the current instrument was re-tuned to stay clearly visible
  against the deeper sidebar.

## Panels sit on the page again
- What's New, Help, Profile, Report a Bug and the Terms and Privacy pages were
  painting the page and the card on it in exactly the same colour, so the panel
  holding the content had no edge and the text appeared to float on the
  background. The page now sits a shade back from the card, the way the
  dashboard already did.
- The "review by counsel" markers on the legal pages were a little too faint to
  read against their own highlight on the dark theme. The highlight is lighter
  now and the text clears the readability standard on both themes.

## The site stops asking twice whether you are signed in
- Opening the site while signed out sent the same "who is this?" request to the
  server twice, because a rejected answer was being retried as though it might
  come back different. Requests that genuinely might succeed on a second try
  still do; a refusal is now taken as the answer the first time.

## Colour, checked across the whole product
- Severity colours — red flag, watch, grounded — were showing the light-theme
  palette everywhere in the dashboard, so warnings read as dark brown on the
  dark background. They now follow the theme on every surface.
- The "Request Access" button on the closing panel had a near-invisible label.
- The gate notice on a deal's Recommendations panel was unreadable.
- The "review by counsel" markers on the Terms and Privacy pages were unreadable
  in both themes.

## Buttons are readable again
- Every primary button in the dashboard — Add milestone, Start diligence, Draft
  with AI, Generate IC memo, Post, Save and the rest — was rendering its label
  in white on a near-white fill, which made it all but invisible on the default
  dark theme. The label now takes the ground colour and flips with the theme.

## The theme no longer flashes on load
- Your chosen theme and motion setting are applied before the page paints. They
  were being blocked in the live build, so every load briefly showed the
  default appearance and then corrected itself — and choosing Still didn't stop
  the opening animation on the first screen.

## Errors about the server say what went wrong
- When the app can't reach its server it now says so plainly — naming the
  address it tried — instead of showing "Failed to fetch" or a message about
  JSON that named the parser rather than the problem.
- Switching between instruments returns you to the top of the new one, which it
  had stopped doing.

## Signing in works from every address
- Signing in from anything other than `localhost` — an IPv6 address, or your
  machine's address on the network when testing from a phone — accepted the
  password and then landed you straight back on the sign-in page, as though it
  had been wrong. The session was being thrown away rather than refused.
- Repeated sign-in attempts are limited to five a minute. That is deliberate,
  but worth knowing: after several quick tries the next one is refused for a
  minute even when everything about it is correct.

## A blank page can no longer swallow the app
- Signing in, or opening any page other than the homepage, could show nothing
  at all — an empty window with no message and no way forward. It happened
  after Ansyra was updated while a tab was left open, and the only cure was a
  refresh nobody was told to make.
- Those pages now recover by themselves. Where they can't, they say what
  happened and offer a way back instead of showing an empty screen.

## The dashboard reports what it is doing
- Panels now hold their shape while they load instead of appearing blank and
  then jumping. A panel that is still fetching says so; a panel that is
  genuinely empty says what would fill it and how to start.
- Saving, deleting and advancing now confirm. Fifty-one writes across the
  product used to succeed in complete silence — including every write on the
  deal page, which had no notification host mounted at all.
- Failures name the problem and what to do about it, rather than showing the
  raw error or nothing.
- The sidebar reads as navigation rather than as another panel, and the
  current instrument is marked by fill, a bar and weight — not by colour
  alone.
- Severity colours got readable cuts for both themes. Watch and grounded were
  light-theme values used unchanged on dark, where they measured around 2:1.
- Still (the motion toggle) now governs the whole app. The dashboard's
  entrance motion answered to the operating system instead of the control, so
  pressing Still left half the surface moving; loading spinners and the
  assistant's thinking dots ignored it entirely.

## Landing page — an honest account of what Ansyra does
- The landing now covers the whole product. Four movements of a deal, from
  origination through to post-close, opening onto all eleven instruments.
  It previously described five.
- New detail pages for Deal Pipeline, Target Screening, Deal Economics, the
  Data Room, Decision Log & IC Memo, and Precedent Comps.
- Every public claim was re-checked against the code. Descriptions that
  promised analysis the product does not perform have been rewritten to
  describe what it actually does.
- The advancement gate is now real: a deal carrying an assumption scored above
  80 with no second-reviewer response can no longer be advanced to a later
  stage. The product said this before; now it enforces it.
- Admins can grant AI Target Discovery and Document Intelligence. The feature
  list in User Management was showing only the 15 default features, so those
  two could never be switched on.

## Phase 15 — Deal execution
- Decision Log: every stage change carries a recorded decision and its
  rationale, and the two are saved together.
- IC memos generated from the deal's own record — assumptions, cultural and
  regulatory reads, economics, document key terms, decision history — with
  anything missing named rather than invented.
- Deal Economics: enterprise value, EV/EBITDA and EV/Revenue, MOIC and IRR
  estimates, and sources and uses. Computed, never AI-generated. Exports to CSV.
- Deal Timeline: milestones, countdowns, and reminder emails before a deadline.
- Precedent Comps: median and quartile multiples from your own closed deals,
  by sector, with low samples flagged as low samples.
- DD Tracker: a living diligence checklist. A document analysis can fill gaps
  in it, and never overwrites a status you set by hand.
- Scenario analysis: base, upside, and downside cases on your assumptions.
- Deal comments: discussion threads on the dossier.

- A step-by-step go-live runbook and smoke-test script mean deploying to a
  real domain is a checklist, not an adventure.
- Database security advisors were reviewed and cleared.

## Phase 10 — Data Room & Document Intelligence
- Attach diligence documents (PDF, DOCX, TXT) to any deal from its dossier.
- One-click AI analysis: executive summary, clause-level red flags with
  verbatim quotes, extracted key terms, and a DD-checklist gap review.
- Documents live in private storage; downloads are access-checked every time.
- Ask your admin to enable "Document Intelligence" to try it.

## Phase 9 — AI Target Discovery
- Describe your acquisition thesis and AI researches the live market for real
  candidate companies — with fit scores, estimates, and cited sources.
- One click adds a discovered candidate to your Targets.
- Ask your admin to enable "AI Target Discovery" to try it.

- Report a bug from inside the app, with screenshots.

## Phase 7 — Roles & access

## Phase 6 — Sign-in & passwords

- Temporary passwords now prompt you to set your own on first sign-in.
